The three problems

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first. Every tenant in #4471 writes the day first. Any date whose day is 12 or lower comes out with day and month swapped: “Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July.” It happens through the app, email and the booking-v2 form. The date the person meant reaches the parser, and the parser flips it. This is the fix that is queued.

hint: the booking-v3 form tells people to type the month first. Its date field says MM/DD/YYYY, so people type the month first. The form reads the tenant’s way, day first. The date is wrong before it reaches the parser, so the parser fix does not touch it. This covers only reports sent through the form at tenants on booking-v3. The same tenants’ app and email reports are parser.

day-shift: dates move by one day. The month stays right. The day moves by one, or one meeting lands on different days in different time zones: “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.” That is time-zone handling, not date order. The desk already read these reports as one-day moves (desk-notes.json), but causeOf sorts by tenant and channel only, so the table on #4471 counts them under parser.

How a report is placed

problemOf in #lib/kb/split.ts, in this order:

  1. The desk read it as a one-day move: day-shift.
  2. Otherwise the cause from causes.ts: hint when it came through a form that prints a month-first hint at a day-first tenant, else parser.

Intake’s tag (meta.intakeTag) is not used. It is a guess from the reporter’s browser: every report from an en-US browser is tagged hint, and every other report is tagged parser, whatever form the tenant runs and whichever way the report came in.

Lessons

  • A subject line says how the report felt to the person who wrote it, not what caused it. Three bugs can share one subject.
  • Before you mark a big ticket known, read a sample of bodies from each tenant and channel.
  • When the desk notes that some reports read differently, that is a new ticket, not a footnote.
